Construction Financial Model — Methodology

Percentage-of-completion WIP, over/under billing, margin fade, AIA-style progress billing, bonding capacity and the covenant ratios a credit agreement actually tests — published from the shipped Methodology PDF.

By Hoda Elmorshidy • Fractional CFO • Financial Modeling • Corporate Finance since 2017 • Published 2026-08-17

This page publishes the methodology behind the Construction Financial Model — a contractor finance model built for the moment a bank or a surety asks for a WIP schedule. Bid estimator, multi-job WIP on the cost-to-cost basis, estimate-at-completion analysis, AIA-style progress billing, working capital and bonding capacity, and covenant ratios, rolled into a one-page bank and surety package. Built for construction, not agencies. The sample contractor and all figures are fictional.

WIP Schedule — Percentage of Completion

Contract Value = Original Contract + Approved Change Orders
% Complete = Cost to Date / Estimated Total Cost  (cost-to-cost)
Earned Revenue = Contract Value × % Complete
Over/(Under) Billing = Billed to Date − Earned Revenue
Costs in Excess of Billings = MAX(Earned − Billed, 0)  → a current ASSET
Billings in Excess of Costs = MAX(Billed − Earned, 0)  → a current LIABILITY

Overbilled means billed ahead of the work — a liability; underbilled is an asset. A shrinking estimated margin (“fade”, a negative Est. GP Change vs Prior) is the first thing a surety underwriter looks for; the Dashboard also counts jobs eroding, flat and improving, so a positive net figure cannot hide erosion on individual jobs.

The standard behind Cost to Date

Under IFRS 15.B19 / ASC 606-10-55-21, an input method must exclude costs that do not depict the transfer of control — wasted materials and significant inefficiencies overstate progress. If Cost to Date exceeds Estimated Total Cost, revise the estimate; the workbook flags that cell.

Progress Billing (AIA-Style)

Completed to Date = Scheduled Value × % Complete
Earned less Retainage = Completed − (Completed × Retainage %)
Current Payment Due = Earned less Retainage − Previous Payments

“AIA-style” describes the schedule-of-values format only — this is not an official AIA G702/G703 form and is not affiliated with or endorsed by the American Institute of Architects.

Working Capital, Bonding & Covenants

Working Capital = Current Assets − Current Liabilities
Aggregate Bonding Capacity = Working Capital × aggregate multiple
Capacity Used = Remaining Backlog / Aggregate Bonding Capacity
Quick Ratio (contractor-adjusted) = (Cash + trade AR) / (Current Liabilities − Billings in Excess)

The bonding multiples are inputs with conventional defaults, not an accounting standard: published surety commentary puts aggregate capacity at roughly 10–20× working capital and a single job at roughly 10–15×, and every surety sets its own. The output is an indicative planning figure — never a commitment or a quote.

The quick ratio deliberately excludes retainage receivable (not collectible until closeout) and costs-in-excess from the numerator, and billings-in-excess from the denominator (that balance settles by performing the work, not by paying cash). Your bank may define it differently — confirm against your credit agreement. Covenant thresholds shown are industry-typical ranges from surety and bank underwriting commentary, not your actual covenant; debt-to-equity is presented for reference because a single typical threshold would be misleading.
