The UAE VAT201 Return, Box by Box

What each box of the FTA's VAT201 return reports, how the output and input tax sections fit together, and when filing and payment fall due — written for UAE business owners and the accountants who file for them.

By Hoda Elmorshidy • Fractional CFO • UAE-Based Practitioner • Corporate Finance since 2017

Every VAT-registered business in the UAE files the same return: the VAT201. It is a single form on the Federal Tax Authority's online portal, and it condenses an entire tax period of sales, purchases, imports, and adjustments into 13 numbered boxes. Most filing errors are not caused by misunderstanding VAT law — they are caused by putting the right numbers in the wrong boxes, or by source data that was never reconciled before submission. This guide walks through what each box reports and how the filing timetable works, as verified against FTA guidance and published tax-advisory summaries as of August 2026.

Who Files, and How Often

A UAE-resident business must register for VAT once its taxable supplies exceed the mandatory registration threshold of AED 375,000; voluntary registration is available from AED 187,500. The standard VAT rate is 5%, with certain supplies zero-rated or exempt. Once registered, the FTA assigns your business a tax period — quarterly or monthly, at the FTA's allocation. Most small and mid-sized businesses file quarterly; larger businesses are typically assigned monthly periods.

The deadline is the part every registrant should memorise: the VAT return must be filed, and any VAT due must be paid, within 28 days from the end of the tax period. Filing and payment share the same window — submitting the return on time but paying late still exposes you to penalties. Returns are submitted electronically through the FTA's portal at tax.gov.ae (EmaraTax).

The Output Tax Section: Boxes 1–8

The first half of the return reports VAT you charged (or must self-account for) on sales and imports — your output tax.

  • Box 1 — Standard-rated supplies. Sales subject to 5% VAT, reported emirate by emirate. The emirate split is why clean transaction records matter: you need to know where each supply belongs, not just the total.
  • Box 2 — Tax refunds provided to tourists. Refunds made to visitors under the tourist refund scheme, which reduce your output tax. Most SMEs outside retail leave this box at zero.
  • Box 3 — Supplies subject to the reverse charge. Where you receive services or goods from abroad and must self-account for the VAT as if you were the supplier.
  • Box 4 — Zero-rated supplies. Supplies taxed at 0% — typically qualifying exports and other zero-rated categories. No VAT is due, but the value must still be reported.
  • Box 5 — Exempt supplies. Supplies outside the VAT charge, such as certain financial services and residential lets. Reported for completeness; no output tax arises, and related input tax is generally not recoverable.
  • Box 6 — Goods imported into the UAE. Import values linked to your tax registration number, drawn from customs records.
  • Box 7 — Adjustments to goods imported. Corrections where the customs-derived figures in Box 6 are incomplete or wrong.
  • Box 8 — Totals. The sum of the output section.

The Input Tax Section: Boxes 9–11

The second half reports VAT you paid on business purchases — the input tax you are entitled to recover.

  • Box 9 — Standard-rated expenses. Recoverable input VAT on domestic purchases and overheads charged at 5%. Only legally recoverable VAT belongs here — blocked categories and VAT attributable to exempt supplies do not.
  • Box 10 — Supplies subject to the reverse charge. The recoverable input-tax side of the reverse-charge amounts you declared in the output section. Declaring the output side in Box 3 but forgetting the matching input entry here is one of the most common self-inflicted overpayments.
  • Box 11 — Totals. The sum of the input section.

The Net Position: Boxes 12–13

  • Box 12 — Total value of due tax. Your output tax for the period.
  • Box 13 — Total value of recoverable tax. Your input tax for the period.

The difference between the two is your net VAT position: if due tax exceeds recoverable tax, the balance is payable to the FTA within the same 28-day window; if recoverable tax is higher, the excess is a refundable credit you can request back or carry forward.

Where Filings Go Wrong

Across UAE close cycles, the recurring VAT201 failure modes are rarely exotic. The value reported in Box 1 does not tie back to the revenue ledger because credit notes were netted in one system and not the other. Reverse-charge services get declared on the output side without the matching input entry. Import figures in Box 6 are accepted without checking them against actual shipment records. And the return is submitted before anyone has reconciled the VAT numbers against the bank — so the first time a discrepancy surfaces is after filing, when correcting it means a voluntary disclosure rather than a pre-submission fix.

Penalties for getting it wrong are set by Cabinet Decision, not by the form itself. As of August 2026 the administrative penalties framework has already changed: Cabinet Decision No. 129 of 2025, published in November 2025 and effective from 14 April 2026, supersedes the previous penalty regime under Cabinet Decision No. 108 of 2021 and moves to a simpler, non-compounding penalty structure across VAT, excise, and tax procedures. The direction of the change is friendlier to taxpayers who correct errors promptly — but the cheapest error is still the one caught before submission.

At a Glance

The UAE VAT201 is the VAT return every registered business files with the Federal Tax Authority. As of August 2026, the standard VAT rate is 5%, the mandatory registration threshold is AED 375,000 (voluntary from AED 187,500), and the return plus any payment are due within 28 days of the end of each FTA-assigned tax period (quarterly for most businesses, monthly for larger ones). The return's 13 boxes cover output tax (standard-rated supplies by emirate, tourist refunds, reverse charge, zero-rated and exempt supplies, imports and import adjustments), input tax (standard-rated expenses and reverse-charge recoveries), and the net payable or refundable position. Administrative penalties are governed by Cabinet Decision No. 129 of 2025, effective 14 April 2026.
