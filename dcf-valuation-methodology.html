WACC — Derived, Not Assumed

Ke = Rf + β × ERP = 4.46% + 1.09 × 4.23% = 9.07%
Kd = 4.90% × (1 − 0.1763) = 4.04%
WACC = 9.07% × 98.6% + 4.04% × 1.4% = 9.00%

Each input is sourced and dated: the risk-free rate is the US 10Y Treasury close of May 12, 2026 (4.46%); the equity risk premium is Damodaran’s implied ERP, January 2026 vintage (4.23%); beta is the 5-year monthly regression vs the S&P 500 (1.09, consensus range 1.08–1.11); the pre-tax cost of debt is a 44bp spread over the 10Y consistent with a AAA/Aa profile. Capital-structure weights use the market value of equity, not book — standard practice per Modigliani-Miller and Damodaran.

Projections — the Normalisation Judgment

Y1–Y2 use actual/consensus data; Y3–Y5 revert to normalised long-term levels (revenue growth 18% → 8%; EBIT margin held at 46%; D&A held at 12.12% of revenue per the 10-K cash flow statement). The FY2026 transitory capex spike (~$150–190B per TrendForce — roughly 46–59% of revenue) is deliberately excluded from the normalised trajectory: the model starts capex at the FY2025 actual of 22.9% — already elevated above the pre-AI average of 13.5% — and tapers to 18%, reflecting permanently higher but post-buildout levels. That is a professional judgment, stated as one, not a consensus forecast.

The honest caveat the model prints about itself

63% of enterprise value sits in the terminal value, so small WACC or terminal-growth changes produce large valuation swings. And Damodaran’s implied ERP (4.23%) is at the low end of the range — a historical ERP of 5.0–5.5% would lift WACC to 9.6–10.0% and cut the implied price to $260–310. The model shows you both; it does not hide the sensitivity.
