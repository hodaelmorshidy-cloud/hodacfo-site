The Model Verification Standard

Before a model is listed for sale, it must clear a set of machine-run integrity gates. This page describes that standard — the same discipline a controller applies to a month-end close, applied to every template in the catalogue.

The gates

Gate 1

Independent golden case

The model’s headline outputs are reproduced by an independent means — a published worked example, a second tool, or a separate hand-built recompute — before anything else is checked. If the file and the independent case disagree, the model does not ship.

Gate 2

Dual-engine formula recompute

Formula cells are recomputed in an independent calculation engine and diffed against the stored values, with coverage reported — any cell the engine cannot genuinely re-derive is flagged as unverified rather than silently passed. A workbook that merely “looks right” in Excel does not pass; the numbers must reproduce outside Excel too.

Gate 3

Reconciliation

Statements must tie: balance sheets balance in every period, cash flows tie to cash, subtotals equal their components, and every “ties out” claim in the file is tested rather than asserted.

Gate 4

Edge & stress inputs

Zero, negative, blank and extreme inputs; non-standard year starts; input validation that must actually catch bad entries. Integrity checks are proven by seeding the exact defect they are meant to catch — a check that has never failed is treated as untested.

Gate 5

Claims audit

Every falsifiable number in a listing — tab counts, formula counts, worked-example outputs — is recomputed from the shipped file. If a claim cannot be verified, it is removed rather than softened.

Gate 6

Adversarial review

The top tier of the standard: an independent reviewer, in a separate environment on different software, instructed to refute the results — with every finding adjudicated one by one. This gate is scoped to flagship models and is the slowest to clear; where a model has completed it, its own product page says so.

What a verification standard cannot do

Scope, stated plainly: gates 1–5 are the build-and-list discipline; gate 6 is a flagship tier that individual models complete on their own timeline, and a model’s own page is the authority on what it has cleared — not this page. Verification also proves only that the model computes what its methodology says it computes. It does not make outputs advice. Results depend entirely on your inputs and assumptions — a verified DCF with optimistic growth assumptions still produces an optimistic valuation. Nothing on this site is investment, tax, accounting or legal advice; defects are covered by the refund policy’s warranty.

What verification looks like in numbers

A standard is only worth the evidence behind it. Across the 11 products that currently publish an audit certificate, the checks below have been run on 38,059 formula cells across 137 tabs — every one of them re-derived from the shipping file itself, not from a build script and not from a copy.

What “formula cell” means here, so you can reproduce the count yourself: an authored formula — every formula element in a worksheet, less the ones Excel generates on its own (array spills and What-If data tables). An .xlsx is a zip archive; unzip it and the worksheets are the XML files under xl/worksheets/. Counting those elements reproduces every figure on every certificate exactly. Two products show the distinction: the Etsy Profit Tracker carries 4,405 formula elements of which 305 are array spills, giving 4,100 authored; the Solar Project Finance Model carries 13,957 of which 6 are data-table cells, giving 13,951. A count you cannot reproduce is not evidence, so the rule is stated rather than assumed.

Two independent engines have to agree before a number is published. Every formula is recomputed in a calculation engine that did not build the workbook, and the headline outputs are rebuilt a second time in Python from the raw inputs alone. When those two disagree, the workbook is wrong until proven otherwise — that is the whole point of running the check twice.

The largest file under this standard is the Solar Project Finance Model, at 13,951 formula cells across 22 tabs. The smallest carries 30. Both clear the same gates: a model is not held to a lower standard because it is small, and the certificate on each product page states exactly what was checked and when.

Eleven of the seventeen products on this site publish an audit certificate today. Each one below carries it on its own page — structure and hash recorded from the shipping file. The other six are named underneath. A certificate appears when that product’s shipping file has been re-verified against the published bytes, and not before — which is why the list is a fraction rather than a total.

What the hash proves, and what it does not. The SHA-256 on a certificate is the digest of the file as published. It lets you prove that the file you downloaded is byte-for-byte the file the certificate describes — that is a check only you can run, after download, and the instructions are on each certificate. It does not prove anything about a copy obtained anywhere else. If a hash does not match, treat it as a defect and say so: that is covered by the Refunds & Fixes policy like any other.
