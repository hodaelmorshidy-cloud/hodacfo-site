The Option Pool Is the Trap

Founder shares, SAFE, three rounds — on paper it looks like arithmetic. It stops being arithmetic the moment an investor asks for a new 12% option pool "created pre-money." That pool comes out of the founders' side of the table, not the incoming investor's, and it has to be solved for simultaneously with the round price — get the order wrong and you either under-dilute the investor or over-dilute yourself. Most templates hard-code one or the other, or spin into a circular reference trying to get both at once.

This model solves the pool shuffle in closed form: expanded pre-money, topped up only when the round actually needs it, no iteration required. In the pre-filled deal, founders go from 84.2% before any outside money to 32.7% at a $200M exit across the SAFE and three priced rounds — and a second, quieter number moves alongside it: whether the preferred stock is participating changes the founders' exit proceeds from $65.4M to $57.9M, a $7.5M swing most cap table tools don't model at all.

The Post-Money SAFE, Converted Correctly

The SAFE follows the Y Combinator 2018 form (valuation cap, no discount). It converts at the first priced round into the greater of the cap-implied share count or what the amount would buy at that round's own price per share — the exact greater-of test in Section 1(a) of the instrument. Most templates hard-code the cap basis alone, which silently under-issues shares to the SAFE holder in any flat or down round and quietly overstates the founders' ownership. Both branches are computed and shown side by side; if the round prices below the cap, the round-price branch wins and the SAFE receives more shares. In the pre-filled deal the $500k SAFE on a $5M cap converts to 1,055,556 shares, about 4.3% of the company at exit. Set the SAFE amount to 0 to skip it entirely.

Two Exit Waterfalls, Not One

Most cap table templates stop at as-converted pro-rata returns, which is the easy 80% of the answer. This model also runs the harder 20%: a liquidation-preference waterfall where each round is independently participating or non-participating, pari passu, and the converted SAFE itself sits in the waterfall as a fifth preferred class carrying the same rights as the round's Standard Preferred — per the SAFE instrument's own definition, not treated as common stock. The workbook ships non-participating by default, so the founder "cost of preferences" reads $0 on first open; toggling one cell on the Liquidation Waterfall tab produces the $57.9M participating case shown above. Both waterfalls tie to the exit value and ownership sums to 100% at every stage — the model's own built-in self-checks.

Common Questions

What deal is pre-filled in the model?

A worked example called Helio Robotics: founders hold 84.2% before any outside money, a $500k post-money SAFE sits on a $5M valuation cap, and three priced rounds follow (Seed, Series A, Series B). At a $200M exit the founders are diluted to 32.7%, the SAFE converts to 1,055,556 shares (about 4.3% of the company), and the blended investor MoIC is 4.71x. It is a fictional company built to be tied out exactly, not a real deal.

Does it handle the option-pool shuffle correctly?

Yes — that is the model's core mechanic. The new option pool is expanded pre-money, so it dilutes existing shareholders rather than the incoming investor, and it tops up only when the round actually needs it (a pool that's already large enough is never re-inflated). Every round is solved in closed form: no iterative calculation, no circular-reference warning, and it survives the trip into Google Sheets intact.

Does it model SAFEs?

Yes — a post-money SAFE using the Y Combinator 2018 form (valuation cap, no discount). It converts at the first priced round into the greater of the cap-implied share count or what the amount would buy at that round's own price per share, per Section 1(a) of the instrument — the branch most templates skip, which silently under-issues shares to the SAFE holder in a flat or down round. Set the SAFE amount to 0 to skip it entirely. Pre-money SAFEs and discount-rate SAFEs are a different instrument and are not modeled.

Does it model liquidation preferences?

Yes, alongside a clean as-converted waterfall. Each round can independently be set participating or non-participating, all pari passu, and the converted SAFE itself sits in the waterfall as a fifth pari-passu preferred class — per the SAFE instrument's own definition, it carries the same rights as the round's Standard Preferred. In the pre-filled deal, founders keep $65.4M under 1x non-participating versus $57.9M once the preferred participate — a swing of about $7.5M the model shows explicitly rather than assuming away. The workbook ships non-participating by default; one cell on the Liquidation Waterfall tab switches it.
