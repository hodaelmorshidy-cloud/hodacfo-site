What Is IRR (Internal Rate of Return)?

The Internal Rate of Return is the discount rate at which a stream of cash flows has a net present value of exactly zero — the implied annual return of an investment, taking timing into account.

IRR = the r solving Σ CFₜ ÷ (1+r)ᵗ = 0.

Illustrative: the IRR winner and the value winner are different projects.

Why it matters

It is the lingua franca of investment decisions — but it is a rate, not a size. A 40% IRR on a tiny, brief project can create less value than 15% on a large, long one.

The practitioner trap

Three classics: cash flows that change sign more than once can have multiple IRRs; IRR silently assumes interim cash reinvests at the IRR itself; and ranking projects by IRR alone starves big-NPV projects. Appraise with NPV at a risk-adjusted hurdle and let IRR describe, not decide — the Capital Allocation System is built around exactly that discipline.
