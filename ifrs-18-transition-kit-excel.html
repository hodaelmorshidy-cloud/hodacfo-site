A Mapping Engine, Not a Blank Template

IFRS 18 replaces IAS 1 for annual periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027. It reclassifies every income-statement line into five categories, introduces two new required subtotals, and requires formal disclosure of the non-GAAP measures you already use with investors. Most templates give you a blank restated P&L and leave the reclassification judgement calls to you — including the two that keep showing up wrong in draft statements.

This kit ships a working Category Mapping engine: enter your existing income statement or trial balance lines, pick an item type for each, and every line is assigned to its IFRS 18 category with the two new required subtotals built automatically. The Two Reclassifications Most Templates Get Wrong

The 2019 exposure draft would have let entities keep equity-accounted associates in Operating if the investee was "integral" to the main business. The final standard dropped that test. Under IFRS 18.53(a) and 55(a), income from associates and joint ventures accounted for under the equity method goes to Investing for every entity, integral or not. In the other direction, a gain or loss on disposing of property, plant and equipment looks like a one-off capital item and gets filed in Investing — but IFRS 18.B48–B49 classifies PPE as an "operating asset," so its disposal result stays in Operating. Both reclassifications move the two new required subtotals; this kit builds them the standard's way from the start, cited to the paragraph.

Covers Banks and Insurers, Not Just Manufacturers

IFRS 18.49 makes entities with a specified main business activity — investing in assets, or providing financing to customers — classify income and expenses differently from everyone else. The Entity Classifier walks through both tests, then reads your classification off a 18-item × 7-archetype matrix (manufacturer, manufacturer with customer financing, retail bank, retail & investment bank, insurer, investment property company, investment entity), each cell cited to its paragraph with every policy choice disclosed. The Bank P&L Example is a full second worked statement showing exactly where a retail bank's customer interest income and funding borrowings land.

The Two Elections That Have to Agree

IFRS 18.56(b)(ii) and 65(a)(ii) are not two independent policy choices — each paragraph ends by requiring the choice to be consistent with the other. Classify interest income on cash one way and the borrowings that fund it another, and the statement is non-compliant, but nothing in a normal template tells you so.

The Entity Classifier handles both sides symmetrically: separate operating and investing election rows on the cash side to mirror the three borrowings rows, each locked to its paragraph, each reading N/A where no choice actually arises. That last part matters — for a retail & investment bank, 56(a) takes precedence, so no 56(b)(ii) choice arises at all and the borrowings election is genuinely free. A live consistency check on the Category Mapping tab flags a mismatched pair, and a dedicated footnote states the rule verbatim with both permitted pairings, so the compliant combination is one you can actually select rather than one you have to argue for.

Common Questions

Is this the official IFRS 18 standard text?

No. It is an independent practitioner template that references IFRS 18 paragraph numbers throughout. Confirm exact wording against the IASB's published standard and your jurisdiction's endorsement status before relying on any citation.

When does IFRS 18 take effect?

For annual reporting periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027, with the comparative period restated. Early application is permitted.

I work at a bank or insurer — does this cover me?

Yes. IFRS 18.49 requires entities with a specified main business activity — investing in assets, or providing financing to customers — to classify income and expenses differently from everyone else.
