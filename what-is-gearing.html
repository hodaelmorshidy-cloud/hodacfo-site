What Is Gearing in Project Finance?

Gearing is debt as a share of total project cost (debt ÷ debt + equity). In project finance it arrives as a hard cap — the maximum share of the project a lender will fund, regardless of how good the cash flows look.

Gearing = senior debt ÷ total capitalisation; the binding constraint is min(sculpted debt, gearing cap × total cost).

Why it matters

Debt sizing has two masters: the DSCR sculpt says how much debt the cash flows can service; the gearing cap says how much the lender will fund. The binding one decides.

The practitioner trap

Assuming the covenant always binds. On the Solar model’s worked deal, sculpting to a 1.40x DSCR supports $134.97M of debt — but the 75% gearing cap cuts it to $120.75M. The cap bound before the covenant did; a model that doesn’t test both constraints ships the wrong debt number.
