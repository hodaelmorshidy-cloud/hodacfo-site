M&A Merger Model — Methodology & Benchmark

Every pre-filled figure sourced from public filings: the CVS/Aetna worked example, the as-initially-reported purchase-price allocation, a five-year no-circularity debt schedule, and the GAAP-vs-cash-EPS lesson at the heart of the model.

By Hoda Elmorshidy • Fractional CFO • Financial Modeling • Corporate Finance since 2017 • Published 2026-08-17

This page publishes the audit trail for the M&A Merger Model (Multi-Year) — a multi-year accretion/dilution model with a five-year debt schedule, EPS bridge, and purchase-price allocation. It is pre-filled with the CVS Health / Aetna acquisition (announced 3 Dec 2017, closed 28 Nov 2018): cash-and-stock, $40bn of new acquisition notes, and CVS’s reported PPA. Modeled on a US-GAAP basis — ASC 805 business-combination accounting, goodwill under ASC 350 (not amortized) — with IFRS 3 / IAS 36 equivalences noted. This is an illustrative reconstruction from public filings, not investment advice; CVS/Aetna do not endorse it.

CVS FY2017 GAAP net income includes a one-time Tax Cuts & Jobs Act benefit (~$1.5bn); the 26% tax rate and zero standalone growth are disclosed modeling assumptions used to isolate deal mechanics.

Purchase-Price Allocation & Amortization

Annual amortization = Σ over intangible classes (fair value / useful life), each class running to the end of its life

The model uses CVS’s allocation as initially reported in the 2018 10-K — customer relationships $13,630M / 14.4yr; provider networks $4,200M / 20yr; technology $1,060M / 3yr; indefinite trademarks $4,100M; VOBA $590M / 20yr — amortized straight-line by their real lives; goodwill is never amortized. Year-1 amortization is ~$1,552M, stepping down after year 3 as the 3-year technology asset rolls off. Because this is a stock deal with no tax step-up, the amortization is a permanent non-deductible book charge (a deductibility flag covers asset / 338(h)(10) deals). CVS later finalized the allocation in its FY2019 10-K; the worked example deliberately retains the initial basis and says so.

Debt Schedule — No Circular Reference

Interest(t) = rate × Beginning(t)
Sweep(t) = MAX(MIN(sweep% × MAX(FCF(t) − mandatory(t), 0), Beginning(t) − mandatory(t)), 0)
Ending(t) = Beginning(t) − MIN(mandatory(t) + Sweep(t), Beginning(t))

Interest is charged on the beginning balance, so every figure in year t depends only on year t−1 — no circularity, no iterative calculation, clean recalculation in Excel and Google Sheets. Mandatory repayments follow the actual note maturities per the CVS 2018 10-K.

The Accretion/Dilution Bridge — the Core Lesson

Pro-forma NI(t) = Acq NI + Tgt NI + after-tax synergies(t) − after-tax interest(t) − after-tax foregone interest − after-tax intangible amort(t)
Accretion(t) = Pro-forma EPS(t) / Standalone EPS − 1

On this trailing-2017, reported-GAAP, zero-growth, no-step-up basis the deal is dilutive: GAAP accretion of −29.7% in year 1 improving to −16.5% by year 5 as synergies ramp, debt pays down and the technology intangible rolls off. On a cash-EPS basis — excluding intangible amortization, the basis acquirers usually guide to — dilution is far smaller: −11.2% to −2.3%. CVS guided to adjusted-EPS accretion on a forward basis. That contrast — GAAP vs cash EPS, trailing vs forward — is the point of the worked example.

Accuracy Standard

Accepted error tolerance: 0.0%. Every formula was recomputed from first principles by an independent Python audit and recalculated in Microsoft Excel with a full dependency rebuild and iterative calculation off. All 208 formula cells match the reference to the cent across both editions, Sources & Uses balances to $0.00, and there are zero Excel error cells.

Not modeled: full three-statement consolidation, target option dilution via the treasury-stock method, tangible-asset step-up and related deferred taxes, refinancing of assumed target debt, revenue synergies. Standalone earnings are held flat by default. Confirm purchase-accounting and tax treatment with your advisers before relying on any figure.
