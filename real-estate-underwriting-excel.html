Real Estate Underwriting in Excel: The Model Structure That Survives a Lender

What a real underwriting model computes — NOI, DSCR, LTV, debt yield, exit — how the pieces constrain each other, and the traps that make deals look better than they are.

Real estate underwriting is the discipline of proving what a property can pay — to a lender sizing debt, to an equity partner sizing returns, and to yourself before wiring a deposit. The Excel model that does it is not complicated in concept; it is unforgiving in construction, because four constraint systems all bind at once.

The four constraint systems

  • Income: gross potential rent → vacancy & credit loss → effective gross income → operating expenses → NOI. Everything downstream is a claim on this line.
  • Debt: lenders size the loan as the minimum of three tests — LTV (value × max leverage), DSCR (NOI ÷ debt service ≥ covenant), and debt yield (NOI ÷ loan). The binding test decides, and it is rarely the one the broker quoted.
  • Equity: what remains after debt funds the basis; returns computed on actual cash in and out, levered and unlevered, with the promote structure applied to the right line.
  • Exit: terminal value from a cap rate applied to a forward NOI — and the honesty test: an exit cap rate below the entry cap rate is a market-timing claim, not an assumption.
The same debt discipline, one asset class over: project-finance models sculpt debt against per-period coverage rather than a single-year test. On the Solar Project Finance Model’s worked deal, the gearing cap binds before the DSCR covenant does — the multi-test minimum in action. Real estate lenders run the identical logic with LTV/DSCR/debt yield.

The traps

1. Underwriting to the offering memo’s NOI

OM NOI is a sales document. Re-derive it: trailing-twelve actuals, mark rents to market only with evidence, and an expense load sanity-checked per unit and per square foot against comparables.

2. A single-year DSCR

Year-one coverage on in-place income says nothing about the refinance year. Model the full hold: rent growth, expense growth, reserve funding, and the rate environment at exit — the deal that works at 1.25x today and breaks at refinancing was never underwritten.

3. Cap-rate arithmetic hiding leverage risk

A 25-basis-point exit-cap move can swamp years of NOI growth. Sensitivity-table the exit cap against rent growth before believing any IRR.

4. The circular reserve

Reserves funded from cash flow that depends on debt service that depends on loan size that depends on… the reserve. Break the loop with an explicit convention, never with Excel’s iterative mode.

What the model must print

NOI bridge · loan sizing with the three tests shown and the binder flagged · levered/unlevered IRR and equity multiple · DSCR path across the hold · sensitivity grids on exit cap × rent growth. If a deal sheet shows one IRR and no constraint analysis, it is a brochure.
