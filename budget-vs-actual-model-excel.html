“We're Ahead of Plan” Is Not an Answer

Every budget template can subtract actual from budget and colour the cell red or green. That number tells you a gap exists. It does not tell you whether you raised prices, sold more units, or simply sold a richer blend of products — and those three cause completely different decisions. Raising price is a pricing conversation. Selling more units is a capacity and pipeline conversation. A favourable mix is often luck that reverses next quarter.

This model separates them properly. In the pre-filled year, revenue is $27,126 ahead of the year-to-date plan — and the decomposition shows $21,339 of that came from volume, only $3,036 from price, with $2,751 from mix. The three components sum back to the revenue variance exactly, and a separate check column re-derives the same figure independently, so the split can't quietly drift out of balance.

What It Deliberately Doesn't Do

Two limits matter most if variance analysis is why you're here. Actual COGS is derived at the budget rate rather than entered, so no separate gross-margin variance appears — and under a scenario with cost inflation other than 1.00, the resulting COGS movement is an artifact of that derivation, not cost performance. The workbook says exactly this on the Actuals tab. Second, the price/volume/mix split is valued at budgeted price (the turnover basis), which is what makes the three components sum to the revenue variance exactly; a contribution-basis bridge answers a different question and isn't included.

Beyond that: the profit line is EBITDA, so there's no balance sheet, no 3-statement build and no interest, tax or depreciation; it's a single entity in a single currency, with no consolidation, no FX and no headcount module; and the Cash Bridge is a working-capital approximation that opens at zero receivables and payables, uses a single-month lag and caps DSO/DPO at 30 days. One operating rule is worth knowing before you buy: enter actuals only through the month set as Current month on Setup — months entered beyond it misstate every year-to-date figure until you advance it, which is why the Actuals tab carries a REVIEW warning that fires exactly when that happens.

If your general ledger only gives you a revenue total for a line rather than units, you can still use the decomposition — back into a units-weighted average price rather than a simple average, which the Actuals tab tells you directly, because a simple average distorts the split.

Common Questions

What exactly is price/volume/mix, and why does it matter?

It answers why revenue moved. Price = you charged more or less per unit. Volume = you sold more or fewer units overall. Mix = the blend shifted toward higher- or lower-priced lines. Those are three different conversations with three different fixes, and a variance column alone cannot tell them apart. In the worked year, a $27,126 revenue beat splits into $3,036 price, $2,751 mix and $21,339 volume — and the three sum back to the revenue variance exactly, with an independent check column proving it.

Do I need to be an FP&A specialist?

No. START HERE gives you a five-step order of operations, and the model opens with a complete worked year you can study before you overwrite it. The methodology PDF explains the method in plain English.

Can I use it for more than one client?

Yes — the multi-client / commercial licence is included by default. Use it internally and to deliver budgeting work for your own clients, including client-branded copies of the outputs. You may not resell the template itself as a template. One purchase covers one practitioner.

Why is the profit line EBITDA and not net income?

The model plans revenue and operating expenses only, so the profit line is earnings before interest, tax and depreciation by construction. It is labelled EBITDA on every tab, with a note on Annual Summary. Add the below-the-line items yourself if you need statutory net income.

Does it work in Google Sheets?

Yes — a second file prepared for Google Sheets is included, formula-identical to the Excel edition. Both use only ABS, CHOOSE, IF, IFERROR, INDEX, INT, MAX, MIN, MOD, OR, SUM, SUMPRODUCT and TEXT — no XLOOKUP, no LET, no LAMBDA, no dynamic arrays, no macros.

Is the AI commentary going to invent numbers?

That is exactly what the figure block is for. The workbook builds a plain-text line of your real figures that updates itself as you enter data — copy it, paste it above the prompt, and the model is writing from your numbers instead of guessing.
