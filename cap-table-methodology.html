Cap Table & Dilution Model — Methodology

The round math, the option-pool shuffle, the post-money SAFE’s greater-of conversion, and how the liquidation waterfall treats a converted SAFE — published from the shipped Methodology PDF.

This page publishes the methodology behind the Cap Table & Dilution Model — a multi-round priced-equity cap table: founder shares, an option pool, up to four priced rounds, one post-money SAFE, and two exit waterfalls. Round Math — Closed Form, No Circular References

Investor % = Investment / (Pre-money + Investment)
Post-round total shares T = Non-pool shares before / (1 − Investor% − Target pool%)
Investor shares = Investor% × T
Option pool (post) = Target pool% × T  (top-up only when needed)
Price per share = Investment / Investor shares

The option pool is expanded pre-money, so it dilutes existing holders, not the incoming investor — and the pool tops up only when needed; a granted pool never shrinks. Existing holders keep their share count; only their percentage falls.

The Post-Money SAFE — the Greater-Of Floor

Safe Price = SAFE post-money cap / Company Capitalization
Shares = GREATER of (Amount / Safe Price) or (Amount / round price per share)

Under the Y Combinator post-money safe, Section 1(a), the safe converts into the greater of the cap-implied share count and what the purchase amount would buy at the new-money price. If the round prices below the cap, the round price wins and the safe holder receives more shares — the cap is a ceiling on price, not a floor. The model computes both branches in closed form and shows them side by side, so you can see which one applied. Conversion keys off the first funded round, not a round named “Seed”.

Pool interaction — the exact YC design

The SAFE % is fixed against the pre-round cap table including the existing option pool, then diluted by both the priced round’s new money and its pool top-up — the round’s pool increase is excluded from the SAFE’s Company Capitalization, exactly as the post-money instrument defines it. Refs: Y Combinator, Primer for the post-money safe v1.1 (2021), and the instrument’s Section 1(a) with the Section 2 definitions.

Exit Waterfalls

As-converted: Proceeds = Ownership % at exit × Exit equity value
MoIC = Proceeds / Invested  ·  Annualized = MoIC^(1/Years) − 1

The Liquidation Waterfall adds preferences across five pari-passu classes — Seed, Series A, Series B, optional Series C, and the converted SAFE. Non-participating classes take the greater of their preference or their as-converted value, solved exactly by an unrolled 4-pass conversion refinement that reproduces the brute-force equilibrium; participating classes take the preference and then share pro-rata. The converted SAFE is treated as Safe Preferred Stock — identical rights and liquidation multiple to the round it converts into, per the instrument’s Section 2 — not as common stock. Both waterfalls tie to the exit value.

The annualized return applies one holding period to every round, so a later round’s true annualized return would be higher than shown — stated on the tab.

Scope — Covered and Deliberately Not

Covers: 1–3 founders; one initial option pool; one post-money SAFE (valuation cap, no discount); up to four priced rounds; the pre-money pool shuffle; 1x+ liquidation preferences with a per-class participating toggle, pari passu.

Not covered: pre-money or discount-rate SAFEs; convertible notes with interest; seniority-ranked (stacked) preferences; participation caps; anti-dilution ratchets; multiple pools; secondary sales; per-round exit dates.
