FP&A Variance & Forecast Toolkit — Methodology

The six-level P&L waterfall, the variance direction convention, the price/volume/mix decomposition and the rolling-forecast architecture — published from the toolkit’s shipped Methodology Notes.

By Hoda Elmorshidy • Fractional CFO • Financial Modeling • Corporate Finance since 2017 • Published 2026-08-17

This page publishes the methodology notes that ship with the FP&A Variance & Forecast Toolkit. They document the P&L architecture, the variance conventions, the PVM bridge and the rolling-forecast engine — for internal use and stakeholder documentation.

The Six-Level P&L Waterfall

Gross Profit = Revenue − COGS
EBITDA = Gross Profit − Operating Expenses
EBIT = EBITDA − D&A
EBT = EBIT − Interest Expense
Tax = MAX(0, EBT × Tax Rate)
Net Income = EBT − Tax

D&A is intentionally excluded from operating expenses and placed below EBITDA — preserving the management-accounting convention where EBITDA represents operating cash generation before non-cash charges, comparable across companies with different capital structures and depreciation policies. The worksheet enforces the position structurally.

The tax line’s stated limitation

MAX(0, EBT × TaxRate) floors tax at zero in loss-making periods — otherwise negative tax would overstate net income. It is a simplified estimate: no deferred tax, loss carryforwards, withholding, or jurisdiction-specific rules. Not for statutory reporting or external audit.

Variance Direction — Sign Convention

All variances are Actual − Budget, everywhere. The arrows and colouring interpret the sign by line type: on revenue and profit lines, positive is favourable; on cost lines, negative is favourable. This is implemented with two Excel number formats — the formula value itself is never changed, so all downstream arithmetic stays consistent:

The PVM Bridge — Three-Way Decomposition

Price Effect = (Actual Price − Budget Price) × Budget Volume
Volume Effect = (Actual Volume − Budget Volume) × Budget Price
Interaction = (Actual Price − Budget Price) × (Actual Volume − Budget Volume)

The three components are mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive — they always sum exactly to the total revenue variance. Using budget volumes in the price term is the algebraically correct choice: actual volumes there would overlap the volume term and the components would exceed the total. The interaction term captures the joint effect, small when changes are modest and material when price and volume move together.

The PVM Bridge carries its own price and volume input grid, by design: the main P&L tabs hold monthly dollar amounts without unit economics, and price-volume decomposition needs unit-level data. PVM revenue totals therefore differ from the P&L tabs — expected, and documented on the tab.

Rolling Forecast Architecture

=IF(month <= ClosedMonth, Actuals, Forecast Inputs)

Every cell in the rolling-forecast table is an IF: closed months pull live actuals; open months reference an editable forecast-input block on the same tab. Changing Closed Through Month in Settings instantly re-partitions actuals vs forecast — no manual overrides, whatever month the buyer starts in. Three named ranges (TaxRate, ClosedMonth, ScenarioLabel) drive every formula tab, so moving a Settings row needs one DefinedName update and zero formula edits.
