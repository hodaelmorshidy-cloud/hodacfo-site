How M&A Deals Actually Move EPS

Purchase price allocation, intangible amortization, and the debt schedule that separates a genuinely accretive deal from a press release that says so anyway — explained by a Financial Controller.

By Hoda Elmorshidy • Fractional CFO • Financial Modeling • Corporate Finance since 2017

“Accretive” is the most overused word in an M&A press release, and it is frequently true only in the first year, only on a non-GAAP basis, or only before the deal's own financing cost is fully reflected. An accretion/dilution model exists to answer one question honestly: after this deal closes, does the combined company's earnings per share go up or down — and why?

The Consideration Structure Sets Everything Else Up

A deal is financed with some mix of cash, new debt, and acquirer stock. The mix matters because each source has a different cost: cash forgone earns nothing further for the acquirer, new debt carries an explicit interest rate, and issuing stock dilutes the existing share count directly. Before a single dollar of synergy is modeled, the consideration mix already tells you most of the story.

Total Consideration = Cash Paid + Value of New Debt + (New Shares Issued × Acquirer Share Price)
Sources = Cash on Hand + New Debt + New Equity
Sources − Uses = 0

Purchase Price Allocation: Where the Premium Goes

The purchase price almost always exceeds the target's book value. Purchase price allocation (PPA) is the exercise of assigning that premium across identifiable intangible assets — customer relationships, trade names, developed technology — each with its own useful life, and whatever is left over becomes goodwill.

Why the intangible schedule matters to EPS

Identified intangibles amortize through the income statement over their useful lives (typically 5–15 years); goodwill does not amortize at all under US GAAP, it is tested annually for impairment instead. A PPA that pushes more of the premium into goodwill and less into amortizing intangibles reports a higher GAAP EPS in the early years — the same deal, the same price, a different-looking result, purely from how the premium was allocated.

The Debt Schedule Carries the Real Cost

Any portion of the deal funded with new debt adds interest expense that did not exist before. A proper accretion/dilution build carries a full multi-year debt schedule — mandatory maturities, a cash sweep against excess free cash flow, interest on the beginning balance so the schedule has no circular reference — not a single flat interest-expense assumption. The debt schedule is what lets the model show whether the deal's accretion is genuinely improving over the projection period or just holding steady while leverage quietly builds.

GAAP EPS vs. Cash EPS: Two Different Questions

GAAP EPS includes the non-cash amortization of the acquired intangibles. Cash EPS adds that amortization back, because it never leaves the bank account. A deal can be dilutive on a GAAP basis in year one purely from intangible amortization and accretive on a cash basis in the same year — and both numbers are correct, they are just answering different questions. A model built to institutional standard reports both bridges side by side for every year of the projection, not just year one.

GAAP EPS Accretion/(Dilution) % = (Pro Forma GAAP EPS − Acquirer Standalone EPS) / Acquirer Standalone EPS
Cash EPS Accretion/(Dilution) % = (Pro Forma Cash EPS − Acquirer Standalone EPS) / Acquirer Standalone EPS
