LBO Model — Methodology

How every number in the multi-tranche LBO model is computed: entry math, the debt stack, the no-circularity interest convention, the six-step waterfall, and the return conventions — published from the shipped Methodology PDF.

By Hoda Elmorshidy • Fractional CFO • Financial Modeling • Corporate Finance since 2017 • Published 2026-08-17

The product page carries a machine-verified audit certificate with the shipping file’s SHA-256.

This page publishes the methodology that ships with the LBO Model — Multi-Tranche. The model executes a full sponsor-driven leveraged buyout: the target is purchased at an EV/EBITDA entry multiple, funded by a multi-tranche debt stack and sponsor equity; a 5-year operating projection feeds a complete debt-paydown waterfall; at exit, sponsor returns are measured by MoIC and a dated XIRR. All sample figures are fictional.

Entry & Sources and Uses

Purchase price is set by an EV/EBITDA multiple applied to entry EBITDA. Transaction fees are added to arrive at Total Uses, and a minimum cash balance is funded at close.

Entry EV = Entry EBITDA × Entry Multiple
Total Uses = Entry EV + Transaction Fees + Minimum Cash
Sponsor Equity = Total Uses − Total Debt Drawn  (equity is the plug)

Debt is sized in turns of entry EBITDA — not as a percentage of EV — so the leverage multiple is directly comparable across deals with different entry prices.

The Debt Stack

Three tranches are modelled, plus a revolver: a Senior Term Loan (highest priority, cash pay, mandatory amortization), Mezzanine (subordinated, cash pay), and a PIK Note (most junior — interest accretes to the balance rather than being paid in cash). The revolving credit facility provides a liquidity buffer against the minimum-cash floor.

Senior Debt = SeniorLev × Entry EBITDA
Mezzanine = MezzLev × Entry EBITDA
PIK Note = PIKLev × Entry EBITDA

Total leverage in the shipped sample is 5.0× EBITDA, within the typical 4–6× range for sponsored mid-market buyouts.

Interest — the No-Circularity Design

All interest is computed on the beginning balance of each tranche. This eliminates the circular reference that arises when interest depends on an ending balance that itself depends on the interest charge — the model resolves in a single pass with no iterative calculation.

The PIK tax caveat, stated on the record

PIK interest is deducted in EBT and added back in the cash flow build as a non-cash item. That accrual deduction is a simplification: under US law a high-yield PIK note can be an Applicable High-Yield Discount Obligation (AHYDO; IRC 163(e)(5) and 163(i)) — part of the deduction can be deferred or permanently disallowed. Confirm deductibility for your instrument and jurisdiction.

CFADS and the Six-Step Waterfall

CFADS = Net Income + D&A + PIK Interest − Capex − ΔNWC

Cash interest on senior, mezzanine and revolver is already inside Net Income; PIK interest is added back because it compounds on the PIK balance instead of consuming cash. CFADS is then applied in strict priority order:

1  Mandatory senior amortization = MIN(Senior Balance, Initial Senior × Amort Rate)
2  Revolver: draw if short, repay excess if long
3  Sponsor distribution (recap) = MIN(Distribution input, excess cash)
4  Senior optional sweep — up to the full remaining balance
5  Mezzanine sweep — after senior is cleared
6  Cash retained ≥ the Minimum-Cash floor

The sponsor distribution is funded before the optional sweep, so cash paid to the sponsor is never also used to pay down debt — distributing cash reduces deleveraging and lowers exit equity, which is the correct economic trade-off of a dividend recap.

Exit & Return Conventions

Exit EV = Exit EBITDA × Exit Multiple
Exit Equity = MAX(0, Exit EV − Net Debt at Exit)
MoIC = (Exit Equity + Cumulative Distributions) / Sponsor Equity
XIRR = rate that NPVs all dated sponsor cash flows to zero

Exit equity is floored at zero because sponsor equity is a limited-liability claim — MoIC cannot go negative, and the Returns tab reports any lender shortfall separately so the depth of a downside case stays visible. XIRR runs on actual dated cash flows (entry at close, distributions on each anniversary, exit at the hold-year anniversary), so it stays exact when interim distributions are non-zero. In a wipe-out scenario both IRR figures floor at −100%; read MoIC alongside IRR — a MoIC below 1.0x is a loss regardless of the IRR shown.

Sensitivity Design

The 5×5 grids sweep MoIC and IRR across entry and exit EV/EBITDA multiples with operating assumptions held constant — isolating the multiple-risk component of returns. The grids use closed-form formulas rather than Excel Data Tables, so they stay Google Sheets-compatible and update without a manual recalculation trigger.

Known Simplifications

Pro-rata debt pricing with no OID, PIK toggles or warrants; no interest income on the minimum cash balance; tax floored at zero with no NOL carryforward; sensitivity grids hold operating assumptions constant; the revolver has no commitment cap.
